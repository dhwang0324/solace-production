# SOL
## Official Character Bible & AI Generation Specification
**Brand:** Solace Productions  |  **Version:** 1.0  |  **Character type:** Original pixel-art robot mascot

**Purpose.** This document is the canonical reference for reproducing SOL consistently in still images, animations, videos, stickers, website graphics, social content, and future 3D/2.5D adaptations. It is based on the **third mascot concept** from the five-design exploration (the rounded white robot concept). The approved name is **SOL**. Any previous exploratory character name (such as “Orbit”) is superseded.

**Design authority.** When visual references and prompt text conflict, follow this order: (1) approved SOL turnaround/reference art, (2) the fixed identity rules in this bible, (3) the current scene brief. The original concept board is a style reference, not a perfectly rigorous engineering diagram; the written rules below resolve minor inconsistencies between its panels.

# 1. One-sentence identity
SOL is a tiny, friendly, premium pixel-art creative robot with an oversized soft-ivory head, a rounded deep-navy digital faceplate, two luminous sky-blue eyes, a single short orb-tipped antenna, symmetrical circular blue side modules, and a small, compact cream-and-slate body with subtle Solace “S” branding.

# 2. Non-negotiable visual identifiers
- **Species / form:** A fully original miniature **robot**, never an animal, person, plush toy, or generic astronaut.
- **Silhouette:** Super-deformed (chibi) proportions; oversized rounded head, narrow torso, very short legs, tiny mitten hands. The overall shape is compact, squat, balanced, and gentle rather than tall or athletic.
- **Head casing:** Almost-white, softly warm ivory shell with rounded corners and low-complexity seams; it reads like a smooth protective hood integrated into the robot—not a helmet placed on a human.
- **Faceplate:** One large horizontally oriented rounded rectangle or soft pill-shaped **near-black/deep-navy display** inset into the front of the head. No nose, snout, fur, hair, eyebrows drawn on the shell, or separate physical face.
- **Eyes:** Exactly two simple, softly illuminated pale-blue digital eye shapes within the screen. Default neutral eyes are short vertical ovals; happy eyes are upward-curving arcs. Eye graphics change expression, but the faceplate itself does not change dimensions.
- **Antenna:** Exactly **one** short, slender, dark-slate antenna extending upward from the top center of the head, ending in a small circular cool-white orb with a soft blue core. Not a horn or an extra ear; never two antennae.
- **Side modules:** One circular headphone-like communication module on each side of the head, part of the robot. Dark slate-blue outer rims, light-blue illuminated inner rings. The Solace “S” may appear on the outward-facing disk where visible.
- **Body:** Tiny off-white torso beneath the head, blue-slate shoulder/torso accents, short segmented arms, rounded paw-free mitten hands, and two short, simple rounded feet. SOL is clearly a machine, not an animal wearing headphones.
- **Brand marks:** A small white or sky-blue **S** insignia on the front chest panel and/or the ear disk. The letter should be upright and legible, not mirrored. Do not cover SOL in multiple logos.
- **Personality in shape:** Soft, inviting, intelligent, unthreatening and slightly whimsical. Never aggressive, militaristic, industrial, frightening, or overly intricate.

# 3. Anatomy and proportions
These are **target ratios for new artwork**, not a claim of exact pixel measurements from the exploratory image.
- **Full standing height:** Measured from feet to top of orb. Use a head-dominant chibi silhouette; the antenna occupies approximately 10–15% of this height.
- **Head-to-body relationship:** Head casing is roughly 1.2–1.4 times the height of the torso-plus-legs, excluding the antenna. From front view, the head dominates the figure.
- **Head width:** About 1.25–1.4 times the width of the torso. Side modules project slightly beyond the head silhouette on both sides.
- **Faceplate width:** Approximately 60–70% of the bare head casing width, with a generous cream border above, below and around it.
- **Faceplate height:** Roughly 40–50% of the main casing height; its corners are broad, never sharp.
- **Eye placement:** Two eye marks placed symmetrically, with a clear dark gap between them. No third eye. Prefer a minimal face without a mouth.
- **Arms:** Two short arms attached at the shoulders. Hands are rounded simplified mittens, not human hands with five individually rendered fingers.
- **Legs:** Two tiny grounded legs/feet, never wheels, treads, floating thrusters, or long humanoid shins unless a separately approved variant is requested.
- **Line weight:** Dark-slate outer pixel outline, consistent around the whole silhouette; interior seams use thinner or lighter steps.

# 4. Exact Solace palette and material rules
Use these **exact brand hex codes** when the model supports them. Treat percentages as approximate art direction rather than measurable requirements.
- **#A5C9E1 — Soft Sky Blue:** Primary glow, illuminated rings, eyes, small screen/interface accents. Approximately 10–15% of the character.
- **#A2A4A5 — Cool Gray:** Small neutral metal details, joint shading, and secondary hardware.
- **#788A95 — Muted Blue:** Transitional casing shadows and subtle rim highlights.
- **#546B7C — Slate Blue:** Side-module housings, shoulder joints, backpack/accessory structures, exterior seams.
- **#3B5560 — Deep Slate:** Strong outlines, shaded joints, hardware, and dark accents.
- **#233036 — Deep Navy:** Main faceplate and deep clothing-free body inserts; use as the screen's principal dark color.
- **#1F2627 — Near Black:** Deepest contrast, selective outline and screen shadow.
- **Supporting neutral:** Soft cream-white / warm ivory shell. Suggested working value **#F6F4F0** (a supplemental neutral, not a new Solace brand color). It should never turn saturated yellow, pure metallic silver, or pink.

**Material finish.** The casing is matte or satin-smooth, like premium molded composite, not polished chrome. Faceplate is low-gloss digital glass. Blue LEDs have a restrained soft bloom, not neon glare. For 2D pixel art, express these finishes through small stepped value changes rather than realistic gradients or photo textures. Any warm gold sparkles belong to the environment, not to SOL’s core palette.

# 5. Fixed geometry by camera angle
## Front view
Centered ivory head, centered navy faceplate, symmetrical luminous eyes, equal round side modules, single top antenna, small torso with understated S chest panel, two visible arms and two small feet. Shoulders are narrower than the head. A clean front view should remain recognizable at favicon/sticker scale.

## Three-quarter view
The preferred default angle for lifestyle images. Turn the robot approximately 25–40 degrees; show the nearer round module fully, the far-side module partially. The faceplate stays integrated into the curved head, not pasted flat onto an unrelated sphere. Keep both eyes visible when possible and preserve one antenna.

## Side profile
The head has a thick, rounded casing around an inset faceplate. The visible side module appears as a circular disk, not a natural ear. A portion of the faceplate is visible wrapping toward the front. Small arms and feet remain proportionate; no human nose or muzzle appears.

## Rear view
Smooth rounded pale casing, no face or eyes on the back; the two circular modules remain aligned at ear height. The antenna projects from the center top. A small blue-slate back panel or compact backpack may display an S, but the backpack is optional and should not be mistaken for permanent anatomy.

## Top / bottom / mirrored views
Preserve the same single antenna, paired side modules, head-to-body proportions and color hierarchy. Do not mirror any visible S lettering; re-render the logo upright rather than flipping the finished image.

# 6. Expression library: screen-only emotion
SOL expresses almost everything through its **two digital eyes**, gentle posture, head tilt, and antenna light. The dark display stays the same size.
- **Neutral / attentive (default):** Two short upright pale-blue ovals; slight head tilt possible; calm, helpful presence.
- **Happy / welcoming:** Two smooth inverted-U arc eyes, warm energetic posture, optional subtle twinkle beside the robot.
- **Focused / working:** Two slightly lowered, narrow/angled eye strokes (not angry brows); body leaned subtly toward the task.
- **Thinking / curious:** One or two rounder eye marks; tiny pause, slight head tilt, or a small external question-mark accent when appropriate.
- **Excited / delighted:** Two small pale-blue four-point stars or bright curved eyes within the faceplate; quick gentle bounce. Star eyes are **temporary**, not a permanent species change.
- **Relaxed / sleepy:** Two closed downward-curving strokes; slight droop; LED orb dims slightly but remains visible.
- **Concerned / empathetic:** Two softened slightly tilted eye lines, still kind and restrained; no exaggerated tears, gaping mouth, or angry emoji face.

**Expression limits:** Never create realistic human pupils, lashes, eyelids, teeth, lips, a nose, beard, hair, eyebrows on the casing, or animal whiskers. A simple optional micro-mouth is discouraged; default to no mouth. Never use more than two eyes.

# 7. Pose, tools and costume permissions
SOL can hold ordinary creative-studio props with simple mitten hands. Camera, lens, clapperboard, sketchpad, stylus, laptop with a plain S, tablet, small map, takeaway mug, tripod, and compact backpack are all permissible **temporary props**. The silhouette must remain recognizable with props removed.

**Creative:** SOL holds a compact black/navy camera with a softly glowing blue ring, looks toward the subject, or adjusts a tripod.

**Design:** SOL sits at a small slate-blue laptop or tablet with an optional S; uses a stylus, drags a pixel window, or thinks beside a tiny plant. Screens should display generic design UI, not illegible text paragraphs.

**Video:** SOL holds a slate clapperboard with a clean white play triangle, or operates a compact camera. Keep both hands attached and clapperboard hinges physically plausible.

**Explore:** SOL carries a small muted-blue backpack and a folded map, or gazes toward a landscape. Backpack, scarf, mug and headgear are props, not permanent identity elements.

**Avoid:** Fashion outfits, hair, animal ears, hats covering the antenna, big shoes, human fingers, weapons, oversized armor, wings, capes, complicated machinery, permanent facial features, or re-colored shells. If a scene requires a costume, it must fit **over** SOL without replacing the canonical head, modules, antenna or faceplate.

# 8. Pixel-art visual language
- **Primary approved medium:** Clean contemporary 2D pixel art with deliberately stair-stepped contour edges, readable pixel clusters and soft two-to-four-tone shading. It should feel handcrafted and modern, not low-effort or heavily pixelated photography.
- **Detail level:** Moderate—enough to show rings, seams and glows, but simple enough to read at small sizes.
- **Edges and shading:** Intentional square/stepped pixels, crisp contour rhythm, dark navy/slate outlines; avoid blurry upscaling, random dither noise and airbrushed edge artifacts.
- **Lighting:** Cool sky-blue rim light; subtle LED glow; ivory body retains shape. Cozy warm environmental light may be used for interiors and sunsets, but never changes SOL’s shell color identity.
- **Backgrounds:** Off-white or pale blue for sprite sheets; deep navy cinematic night scenes for website hero art; restrained pastel landscapes for exploration scenes.
- **Consistency across art modes:** For 2.5D or 3D animation, translate the *same* silhouette, proportions, colors and digital eye expressions. Do not reinterpret SOL as a sleek humanoid robot, chrome droid, toy animal or astronaut.
- **Separation:** SOL should remain visually legible against both white and near-black backgrounds; preserve a dark outline or subtle contrasting edge where needed.

# 9. Character personality and motion language
**Role:** The quiet creative companion of Solace Productions: helpful, observant, curious, inventive, reassuring, lightly playful. SOL communicates calm competence rather than loud comedy or hyperactive marketing energy.

**Idle:** Gentle 1–2 pixel-equivalent vertical breathing/bob at native sprite scale; rare small head tilt; intermittent screen-eye blink; soft orb pulse. Feet remain planted unless walking is specified.

**Walk:** Short deliberate steps with light torso bounce and subtle alternating arm swing. No skating, leg stretching, floating or impossible speed changes. Keep antenna, eye marks, screen and side modules anatomically attached in every frame.

**Gestures:** A modest wave, nod, glance, lean, contemplative head tilt, careful two-hand grip on equipment, brief celebratory micro-hop. No rubber-hose deformation, sudden limb multiplication or elastic faceplate morphing.

**Eyes:** Expression changes should be a deliberate screen animation, not random re-drawing. A blink closes both eye symbols briefly and returns to the intended expression. The display does not sprout a mouth, eyebrows or new interface during a gesture.

**Antenna:** The glowing orb remains on the tip of the **same one antenna**. Small bob or light pulse is permitted; no independent flying orb or second antenna.

**Audio / voice:** SOL can be silent. If a voice is added in a specific project, use a warm, gentle, curious tone and minimal dialogue; no synthetic harshness. Voice is not a visual requirement.

# 10. Default production specifications
- **Reference lock:** Always upload/attach the official SOL concept image or approved clean turnaround where the generation tool permits. Text alone will not guarantee pixel-perfect repeatability.
- **Default image orientation:** 1:1 or 4:5 for character renders, 16:9 for web hero scenes, transparent PNG for sprite assets, and appropriately scaled sprites for animations.
- **Default video:** 16:9 landscape, 24 fps, 5–8 seconds per shot, one readable action per shot, clean 2D pixel-art animation, smooth deliberate motion without faux 3D camera deformation. Adapt to 9:16 only for vertical campaigns.
- **Camera:** Static, slow push-in, or mild lateral move. Avoid fast orbits around the character unless a complete locked model/turnaround exists.
- **Framing:** Keep antenna and feet in frame for full-body shots. Leave clear negative space for text when used in a website hero.
- **Effects:** Small cyan pulses, a few square sparkles, simple interface cards and gently stepped shadows; no explosive particle systems or strong lens flares covering SOL.
- **Asset output:** Export stills as lossless PNG when possible; use a consistent pixel scale (nearest-neighbor for resizing); preserve an alpha channel for transparent assets. For video, request a clean stable image and avoid compression that blurs pixel edges.

# 14. Example prompts / approved scenes

# 15. Multi-image & animation consistency workflow
1. **Lock the anchor:** Save the approved concept art and produce one clean, neutral front-view transparent master of SOL. Review it before expanding the asset set.
2. **Create the model sheet:** Front, three-quarter left, side, back and three-quarter right, all from the same master. Verify antenna count, faceplate geometry, side modules, body proportions and S orientation.
3. **Generate the expression sheet:** Keep the head and screen fixed; change only eye glyphs and micro-posture. Approve neutral and happy before generating the rest.
4. **Generate the pose set:** Build standing, walking, waving, sitting, holding a camera, using a laptop, clapperboard pose, and backpack pose from the approved character reference.
5. **Create motion tests:** Start with 3–6 second single-shot loops (idle, blink, wave, walk) before trying complex scenes or multi-shot videos.
6. **Review against the checklist:** If any non-negotiable element changes, regenerate or correct that frame—do not accept gradual identity drift.
7. **Version assets:** Suggested pattern `SOL_v1_front_neutral.png`, `SOL_v1_happy.png`, `SOL_v1_idle_6s_24fps.mp4`, `SOL_v1_explore_16x9.png`. Keep approved exports separate from experiments.

# 17. Final governing rule
**Do not redesign SOL to fit a scene. Design the scene, prop and movement around SOL.** Every asset should be recognizable as the same Solace Productions character even with no logo, text, backdrop or accessories. When in doubt, prioritize the approved reference, simple geometry and on-model consistency over novelty.
